Homemade Pizza

By Laura Bennett · Published 2026-07-18 · 16g protein per serving

Homemade pizza gets better the moment you start preheating the pan or stone along with the oven — that blast of bottom heat is the difference between a crispy crust and a soggy one.

Beyond that, it's just assembly, and it's faster than waiting for delivery most nights.

Homemade Pizza

Prep: 20 min Cook: 15 min Total: 35 min Yield: 2 pizzas (4 servings) 380 cal · 16g protein

Ingredients

  • 1 lb pizza dough (store-bought or homemade), room temperature
  • 1 cup pizza sauce
  • 2.5 cups shredded mozzarella
  • Toppings of choice (pepperoni, mushrooms, peppers, etc.)
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • Cornmeal, for dusting

Instructions

  1. 1. Preheat

    Preheat oven to 475°F (245°C) with a pizza stone or baking sheet inside to heat up.

  2. 2. Shape the dough

    On a lightly floured surface, stretch or roll the dough into a 12-inch round. Transfer to a pizza peel or parchment paper dusted with cornmeal.

  3. 3. Add toppings

    Brush the edges with olive oil. Spread pizza sauce evenly, leaving a border for the crust. Top with mozzarella and desired toppings, then sprinkle with oregano.

  4. 4. Bake

    Slide the pizza onto the hot stone or baking sheet. Bake for 12-15 minutes until the crust is golden and the cheese is bubbly and lightly browned.

Tips & Common Questions

Why preheat the pizza stone or pan?

A hot surface gives the dough an immediate blast of heat from underneath, which is what creates a crispy crust bottom — putting dough on a cold pan results in a pale, soft base.

Why let the dough come to room temperature first?

Cold dough straight from the fridge is stiff and resists stretching, tearing easily — room temperature dough is far more pliable and easier to shape without holes.

How do I avoid a soggy center?

Don't overload with sauce or watery toppings (like fresh tomato slices) — use a moderate amount of sauce and pat very wet toppings dry before adding them.
