Brown Butter Chocolate Chip Cookies

By Laura Bennett · Published 2026-07-17 · 2g protein per serving

Browning the butter is the single change that takes a good chocolate chip cookie to a genuinely great one — the milk solids toast in the pan and add a nutty, caramel-like depth that regular melted or softened butter can't match.

It adds maybe 10 extra minutes to the process, mostly hands-off, for a cookie that tastes noticeably more complex.

Jump to Recipe ↓

Watch the butter closely once it starts foaming — it goes from perfectly browned to burnt in under a minute, and burnt butter tastes bitter, not nutty.

Brown Butter Chocolate Chip Cookies

Prep: 20 min Cook: 10 min Total: 30 min Yield: 20 cookies 190 cal · 2g protein

Ingredients

  • 1 cup unsalted butter
  • 2 1/4 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 tsp baking soda
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 3/4 cup packed brown sugar
  • 1/2 cup granulated sugar
  • 1 large egg plus 1 egg yolk
  • 2 tsp vanilla extract
  • 2 cups chocolate chips
  • Flaky sea salt, for topping (optional)

Instructions

  1. 1. Brown the butter

    Melt the butter in a light-colored saucepan over medium heat, swirling occasionally, until it foams, then turns golden brown and smells nutty, about 5-7 minutes. Pour into a heatproof bowl to cool for 15-20 minutes.

  2. 2. Mix dry ingredients

    Whisk together flour, baking soda and salt in a bowl.

  3. 3. Combine wet ingredients

    Whisk the cooled brown butter with both sugars until combined. Whisk in the egg, egg yolk and vanilla until smooth.

  4. 4. Combine and chill

    Fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture until just combined, then fold in chocolate chips. Chill the dough for at least 30 minutes — this helps the flavor develop and prevents excess spreading.

  5. 5. Bake

    Preheat oven to 375°F (190°C). Scoop dough onto a lined baking sheet, spaced 2 inches apart, and bake for 9-11 minutes until the edges are set. Sprinkle with flaky salt if using.

Tips & Common Questions

How do I know when the butter is browned, not burned?

Watch for the color to shift to an amber-gold and the smell to turn nutty rather than sharp — it happens quickly after the foaming stage, so stay at the stove and swirl the pan constantly toward the end.

Why chill the dough?

The butter is liquid after browning rather than creamed cool and soft, so chilling solidifies it again — skipping this step results in cookies that spread thin and greasy.

Can I skip the extra egg yolk?

The extra yolk adds richness and chewiness that a single whole egg doesn't fully replicate — it's a small but noticeable difference in texture.
